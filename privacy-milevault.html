MileVault

Privacy notice  ·  Effective August 2, 2026  ·  Vibesoft LLC

We do not collect your data. Nothing about you is sent to a Vibesoft server, because there is no Vibesoft server. There is no account, no analytics, no crash reporter, and nothing for us to breach, sell, or be compelled to hand over.

The only circumstance in which anything leaves your device is when a feature you are using cannot work otherwise. In those cases we send only the minimum that feature needs, only to the party providing it, only for as long as the request takes, and we attach nothing that identifies you or your install.

In MileVault there are exactly three such circumstances, and they are not the same kind of thing as each other:

  • Checking the IRS rates — on by default. The app asks irs.gov for a public page. Nothing about you goes with it.
  • Maps and street addresses — off by default. Coordinates you drove to are sent to Apple, in exchange for a map tile or an address.
  • iCloud sync — off by default. A copy of your log is kept in your own iCloud account. Not collection — it is your storage.

Everything MileVault is for — detecting drives, classifying them, calculating your deduction, exporting a log — works fully with all three switched off.

What MileVault stores, and where

To log your mileage, the app records, on your device: the start and end coordinates of each drive and the distance between them; the date, time and duration; the purpose you assign; any note, vehicle or place name you type; your odometer readings if you enter them; and the path you drove, only if you turn on route recording.

All of it lives in a single file inside MileVault's private storage on your iPhone, protected by iOS file encryption. That file is excluded from automatic iCloud and iTunes backups — a backup would put a copy of your movements somewhere we cannot make promises about. Exporting is how you make a copy on purpose.

1. Checking the IRS mileage rates

This is on when you install the app, and it is the only thing here that is. It is on because it discloses nothing, so there is nothing for you to consent to.

Your deduction is calculated from the IRS standard mileage rates, which change — sometimes mid-year, as they did on July 1, 2026. MileVault ships with those rates built in, and once a week it asks the IRS standard mileage rates page whether a newer one has been published.

What that request contains: no trips, no coordinates, no distances, no dates you drove, no account, no device identifier, no advertising identifier. No cookies — the request is made with cookie storage disabled and cookie acceptance switched off, so irs.gov cannot set one that would come back on next week's check and identify this install. We add no identifying headers of our own.

It is a request for a public government web page, identical for every user of MileVault. What the IRS learns is that some device asked for a page anyone can open in a browser. As with any request over the internet, your IP address is visible to the server you contacted; we send nothing beyond it.

What the check cannot do is change a rate the app already has. A newly published rate can only be added to the end of the table. A rate already on file is never modified and never removed, whatever the page says, because people amend prior-year returns and last year's figure has to stay reproducible. If the page ever disagrees with a rate MileVault holds, the app refuses to apply it and shows you that it happened.

Every report and export states which rates it used, the date the newest one took effect, and whether it came from the app or from irs.gov. Turning the check off is fine — the rates built into your version are used instead, and your deduction is calculated exactly as before.

2. The two features that send something about you

Both ship off. Each states what it sends and to whom on the screen where you turn it on. Neither is retroactive: switching one on changes what happens next, never what already happened.

Show routes on a map

MileVault starts saving the path of each new drive, not just its start and end, in the same file on your device under the same protections. Drawing that path downloads map tiles from Apple for the area you drove in, so Apple learns roughly where you are looking at a map, as it would for any app that displays one.

Drives recorded before you turned it on have no path saved and will never gain one — those points were discarded as they arrived. Turning it back off stops new paths being saved immediately, not at the end of the current drive.

Look up street addresses

MileVault sends the start and end coordinates of a trip to Apple's geocoding service, at the moment you open that trip, and receives a street address in return.

  • Only the two endpoints. Never the route, even when routes are being recorded.
  • Only for trips you actually open. Turning the setting on does not sweep your history sending off a year of your movements in one burst.
  • Addresses are stored on your device once received, so the same place is not looked up repeatedly.
  • Names you gave places yourself always take precedence, and cost nothing.

MileVault attaches no account, name, or identifier of its own to these requests. What Apple's service transmits and retains is governed by Apple's privacy policy, not ours; we are not in a position to make promises on Apple's behalf and are not going to pretend otherwise. A coordinate cannot be anonymised and still be geocoded — the coordinate is the request — which is exactly why this one is off until you decide it is worth it.

3. iCloud sync: your storage, not ours

This is not collection. If you turn it on, a copy of your vault is placed in your own private iCloud account, in MileVault's app container. It is your storage, your Apple Account, and a copy you asked for. Vibesoft has no account, no access, and no way to read it. It never touches a server of ours, because there isn't one.

The file is stored in the app's private area of the container, not the Documents area, so it does not appear in the Files app and cannot be shared by accident.

  • Turning it on copies your whole existing log, and says so before it does anything, naming the number of trips. A sync that started from today would produce a cloud copy missing every drive you had already taken, which is not a backup of anything. This is the one switch whose effect reaches backwards, and it is made loud for that reason.
  • With Advanced Data Protection enabled on your Apple Account, Apple cannot read the file either. Without it, Apple holds the keys and can be compelled to produce it. That is the part "iCloud" quietly glosses over, so it is said here plainly.
  • Turning it off offers to delete the iCloud copy, and tells you if that did not work.

Location and motion

MileVault asks for location access — including background access, if you grant it — solely to measure how far you drive. Location data is used to compute distances and is stored as described above. It leaves your device only through the two optional features in section 2, and through iCloud sync if you enable it. The IRS rate check sends no location of any kind.

The app also reads your iPhone's motion activity classification — driving, walking, or still — to tell drives apart from other movement and to avoid running GPS when you aren't in a car. This is used in the moment and is neither stored nor transmitted.

Exports and purchases

When you export a CSV or PDF, that file is created on your device and goes only where you send it. If you email it, message it, or save it to a cloud service, it is then subject to that service's terms, not ours.

MileVault is sold as a one-time purchase with no subscription. Payment is handled entirely by Apple; we never see your payment details, and Apple's privacy policy governs that transaction.

Analytics, tracking, and advertising

There are none. MileVault contains no analytics SDK, no crash reporter, no advertising identifier, and no third-party tracking code of any kind. Nothing you do in the app is measured or reported, to us or to anyone.

This is true regardless of which features you have enabled. The three circumstances above are the complete list of times MileVault opens a connection: asking the IRS for a public page, asking Apple for a map tile or an address, and putting a file in your own iCloud. There is no fourth.

Deleting your data

Settings → Delete all data erases every trip, vehicle, and place from your device immediately, and — if you turned on iCloud sync — the copy in your iCloud account too. Deletion is permanent. There is nothing for us to delete on our end, because we never had it.

If sync is on and the iCloud copy cannot be reached at that moment, MileVault says so rather than reporting success. A delete that quietly left a year of your movements in iCloud would be the worst thing this app could do.

Deleting the app removes the copy on your device. If you used sync, remove the iCloud copy first with Delete all data, or from iCloud settings on your Apple Account.

Children

MileVault is a business tool and is not directed at children. It collects no personal information from anyone, of any age.
