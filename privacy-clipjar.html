Clipjar

Privacy notice  ·  Effective November 20, 2026  ·  Vibesoft LLC

Clipjar collects nothing. No analytics, no crash reporting, no advertising, no third-party SDK, and no account to create. Its App Store privacy label reads Data Not Collected, in every category.

Where your recipes live

On your device. If you have iCloud turned on, they also sync to your own private iCloud database, the same way your Notes and Reminders do — Apple stores them in your account, under your Apple ID.

We operate no server. There is no Clipjar account and nothing to sign up for. Your recipes are never sent to us, because there is nowhere for them to be sent.

Photographs

A photo you take or choose — a recipe card, a cookbook page — is read on your device and kept with the recipe, on your device and in your iCloud. It is never uploaded anywhere else.

When you add a recipe from a link, Clipjar keeps a small copy of the dish’s photo, if the page names one, for your own reference. You can turn this off in Settings › Importing. A website’s photo is never included when you share or export a recipe.

Network access

Clipjar goes online for one thing of its own: when you hand it a link — by pasting it, dropping it on the window, or sharing a bare link from another app — it downloads that page, and the dish’s photo, so it can read the recipe. The same happens when you open a recipe someone shared with you from a website: Clipjar downloads the method from the original page. The request goes directly to the recipe’s website, uses a standard Safari browser identification with “Clipjar” added, carries no cookies or stored identity, and passes through no server of ours. Nothing about it is recorded.

When you share a page from Safari, Safari hands Clipjar the page you are already reading, and Clipjar makes no request at all. Photos, PDFs, files and pasted text are read entirely on your device.

Otherwise, only Apple’s services use the network, outside the app: iCloud sync between your own devices, and buying the unlock through the App Store. Apple handles the payment; we never see your name, email address or payment details.

More from Vibesoft (in Settings) opens a page listing our other apps in your browser. That is your device opening a page when you ask it to; Clipjar sends nothing.

Sharing a recipe

When you share a recipe, it travels inside the link itself. The link points to a page on vibesoftllc.com, but the recipe is in the part of the link after the “#”, which web browsers never send to a server — so our website cannot see what you shared, or with whom. No photos are included. If the person you send it to has Clipjar, the link opens the recipe in their app. If not, the page shows them the recipe’s title, credit and ingredients, read from the link by their own browser and sent nowhere, and suggests getting Clipjar. A recipe that came from a website travels as its title, ingredients and a link to the original page, not as a copy of the site’s instructions; when it arrives, the recipient’s Clipjar downloads the method from that page, the same way it would for a link they pasted themselves.

Cooking timers

A timer’s alert is a notification scheduled on your device by the operating system. No push server is involved, and nothing is sent anywhere.

Recipes from websites

An imported recipe keeps a link back to where it came from and the date you saved it. Your collection is private to you: Clipjar has no way to publish recipes, and no server on which they could be published.

Exports

A Markdown or PDF file you export is a file on your device. Where it goes next is up to you — Clipjar hands it to the system and takes no further part. Exporting is never locked behind the purchase.

Deleting

Deleting a recipe removes it, and its photos, from this device and from your iCloud. Because there is no copy on a server of ours, that is the end of it.

Children

Clipjar collects no personal information from anyone, of any age. It has no accounts, no chat, and no social features.
