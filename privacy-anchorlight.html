Anchorlight

Privacy notice  ·  Effective November 18, 2026  ·  Vibesoft LLC

Anchorlight collects nothing. That is the whole policy, but this app looks at every device on your network, so here is exactly what happens to what it finds.

Its App Store privacy label reads Data Not Collected, in every category. Settings › Privacy in the app says the same thing in brief.

Where your roster lives

On your Mac, in the app’s own storage. There is no sync, no iCloud container and no server of ours. There is no Anchorlight account, nothing to sign up for, and no email address collected. Your roster is never sent to us, because there is nowhere for it to be sent.

How devices are identified

Other network scanners name your devices by uploading a fingerprint of each one to a database the company owns. Anchorlight doesn’t. It identifies devices on your Mac, from what the devices themselves say:

  • the hardware address, matched against the IEEE vendor registry, which is a table bundled inside the app;
  • what each device announces over Bonjour on your local network;
  • which ports it answers on, and what the service on that port says when asked;
  • its name on your local network.

Every one of these is a question asked of a device on your own network. None of it is sent anywhere else.

Network access

A scan never leaves your local network. Anchorlight talks only to devices on the network your Mac is connected to, and only as a client. It asks; it never tries a password or a login.

The app goes beyond your local network in exactly these cases, and each one happens only when you ask:

  • Updating vulnerability data. Settings › Vulnerabilities shows each source and the host it contacts. Nothing is fetched until you press Update. The built-in sources are the US National Vulnerability Database (services.nvd.nist.gov) and CISA’s Known Exploited Vulnerabilities list (cisa.gov). The request asks about a fixed list of products that is the same for every user; it says nothing about the devices on your network. Those servers see an ordinary web request from your Mac, as they would from a browser. If you add a source of your own, the app contacts that address when you press Update.
  • Buying the unlock, through Apple’s App Store. Apple handles the payment; we never see your payment details.
  • Links you click. Opening an advisory on nvd.nist.gov, a device’s own web page, or our support email hands the link to your browser or mail app. Anchorlight doesn’t record that you did.
  • More from Vibesoft (Settings › About) lists our other apps and websites. Clicking a row opens that app’s App Store page, or the website, in the App Store or your browser. The list is built into the app and fetches nothing; Anchorlight doesn’t know which row you clicked, keeps no record of it, and has nowhere to send one.

What Anchorlight does not do

  • No analytics. No usage statistics, no crash reporting, no telemetry.
  • No account, no sign-in, no email.
  • No advertising, and no third-party SDKs of any kind.
  • No tracking, in the App Store’s sense or any other.
  • No subscription, so there is no billing relationship and no renewal to lapse.

Exports

A CSV or PDF you export is a file on your Mac, saved where you choose. Where it goes next is up to you. Anchorlight takes no further part.

Deleting everything

Delete a saved network from the sidebar (Delete Network…) and its roster and scan history are gone. Everything else the app keeps — your settings and any vulnerability data you downloaded — is in its folder at ~/Library/Containers/com.vibesoftllc.anchorlight; moving the app to the Trash leaves that folder behind, so delete it too if you want nothing left. Because there is no copy on a server of ours, that is the end of it.

Children

Anchorlight collects no personal information from anyone, of any age. It has no accounts, no chat, no social features, and nothing it finds leaves your Mac.
