For content owners

Clipjar  ·  Effective November 20, 2026  ·  Vibesoft LLC

Clipjar is a recipe manager. If you write, photograph or publish recipes and something about how Clipjar handles your work concerns you, email dev@vibesoftllc.com. A person reads every message.

What Clipjar does with a recipe page

A Clipjar user saves a recipe into their own private collection, on their own devices and in their own iCloud. There are two ways a page gets in:

  • Shared from Safari. Safari hands Clipjar the page the user is already reading. Clipjar makes no request of its own.
  • From a link. When a user pastes, drops or shares a bare link, Clipjar downloads that one page, once, directly from your site, without cookies or a login. It does not get past a paywall or a sign-in. Its request uses a standard Safari browser identification with Clipjar/1.0 added at the end, so it appears in your logs as a Safari visitor that names Clipjar.

Vibesoft runs no recipe server: we hold no copy of any recipe, cannot see anyone’s collection, and publish nothing.

  • Credit. A recipe saved from your site keeps your page’s address and the date it was saved, and shows your site’s name and the author where your page’s recipe markup gives them. The app has no way to edit the source fields.
  • Photos. When a recipe is added from a link, Clipjar also downloads the dish photo your page names and keeps an 800-pixel copy with the recipe. This is on by default and the user can turn it off. A recipe shared from Safari gets no photo. Website photos are never included when a recipe is shared or exported.
  • Sharing. When a user shares a recipe that came from a website, the link carries its title, ingredients, tags and your page’s address, but not your written method, summary or the user’s notes. The recipient’s Clipjar then downloads your page, the same way as a pasted link, to fill in the method. That is a download by the app, not a browser visit; your ads and page scripts don’t run.

What we can do if you ask

  • Stop downloading your pages. We add your domain, and its subdomains, to Clipjar’s do-not-download list in the next app update. Once a user has that update, Clipjar downloads nothing from your domain: no page, whether they paste a link or open a recipe shared with them, and no photo, even when another site’s page uses one of yours. A link to your site is saved as a link, for them to open themselves.
  • Look at how you’re credited. If your site name or author comes out wrong, tell us what your pages should show. Where Clipjar is reading your page wrongly, we fix it in an update.
  • Answer questions about anything else Clipjar does with public recipe pages.

What we can’t do, stated plainly:

  • The list takes effect only in app updates, and only for users who install them.
  • It doesn’t cover a page a user shares from Safari, because that is their own browser handing over a page they are reading, the same as saving it from the browser.
  • Because Clipjar has no server, we can’t reach into a user’s device or iCloud to delete a recipe they have already saved.

What to include

The site or pages concerned, what you would like us to do, and how we can confirm you speak for the site (an email from its domain is usually enough). There is no form to fill in and nothing to sign.
